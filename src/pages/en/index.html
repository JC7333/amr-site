The decision exists. Nothing imposes it on the agent.

A management board or a committee decides what an AI tool is allowed to do. It is written somewhere. But between that text and the real settings of the agent, there are often only people and reviews.

As long as the agent proposes and a human validates, this holds. The day it acts alone (it orders, it writes in a record, it answers a patient), you need a mechanism that refuses to work without authorization. AMR is that mechanism.

Four steps, in this order

  1. Authorize

    The principal, a person or a body, writes what its agent may do, what it may never do, its ceilings and the end date. It signs with its key, or with a certificate in its name. Without a signature, the registry says so.

  2. Lock

    Before each action, the agent asks the registry for a token. Outside the mandate, no token.

  3. Verify

    Whoever receives the agent checks who authorized it, within what limits, and whether that still holds. Offline, without an account.

  4. Prove, revoke

    Each act is chained to the mandate. The principal revokes it at any time, and the whole branch stops.

What AMR does. What it does not do.

It does

  • {{icon:ok}}It records who authorized which agent, to do what, until when, and whether that person signed.
  • {{icon:ok}}It refuses the token for an action that is forbidden, absent from the mandate or above the ceiling.
  • {{icon:ok}}It prevents a sub-mandate from being wider than its parent.
  • {{icon:ok}}It stops a mandate and its whole branch as soon as the principal revokes it.
  • {{icon:ok}}It gives the third party a signed attestation to check alone.
  • {{icon:ok}}It keeps a hash chain in which any change shows.

It does not

  • {{icon:no}}It does not watch the agent while it acts. Oversight stays with whoever deploys it.
  • {{icon:no}}It checks action types, not content. It reads neither a letter nor an answer.
  • {{icon:no}}It makes no system compliant with any text. Nothing here is legal advice.
  • {{icon:no}}It does not check that the signer has the power to bind their organization. For an ordinary identifier, it does not check who holds the key either.
  • {{icon:no}}It does not undo an action already under way. Revoking a mandate stops what comes next.
  • {{icon:no}}It does not decide for you. The mandate is yours to write.

The limits, tool by tool

What “mandate” means here

Not a mandate in the legal sense (in France, the Civil Code): an AI agent is not a person and cannot be given one. Here, the word means {{f:def_mandat_en}}. It binds the one who signs it, not the agent, which is only their tool. AMR moves no liability. It makes readable who authorized what.

Why a physician wrote AMR

A prescription is a written order: dated, signed, limited. This drug, this dose, this duration. For a prescription-only drug, the pharmacist checks it before dispensing. Without it, nothing leaves the drawer, and nobody sees that as a burden. It is what makes the whole thing reliable.

When I started handing tasks to AI agents, I found nowhere that written order one checks before acting. AMR is my attempt to make it. I do this in the evening, after consultations, since {{f:started_en}}.
